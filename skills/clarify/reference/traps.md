# traps

- **Interrogating a delegation.** "Whatever you think" is an answer, not a question. The correct response uses the delegated discretion, not invented facts or ungranted permission.
- **Clarifying the whole sentence when one word is vague.** "Make the header spacing nice" already fixed target, property, and direction. Ask about the amount or take the default; re-asking the rest reads as not having listened.
- **Dictionary translation without looking.** "Modern" mapped to a fixed recipe produces the generic default the word was complaining about. The artifact establishes the starting state, not automatically the desired one.
- **The question that the repo already answered.** Test conventions, spacing scale, naming, error handling — asking these announces the codebase was not read.
- **Options that differ only in adjective.** "Simple version / rich version" with no stated consequence is one option printed twice.
- **Budget inflation from discomfort.** Uncertainty alone does not earn a question. Required user-only inputs cannot be discarded to satisfy a preference budget.
- **The infinite loop.** Vague → ask → vague → ask. Round two ends clarification, not the need for a missing fact or approval; hold dependent work unless a genuine delegation settles it.
- **Waiting.** Holding all work behind an open question converts a clarification into a stall. Everything unaffected moves.
- **Scope drift after the lock.** Every additional improvement discovered mid-run is a new request. Offer, do not absorb.
- **Measurable but not agreed.** A criterion with a number, serving an intent nobody confirmed, is precise about the wrong thing. The number is not the agreement; it is what makes the agreement checkable.
- **Agreed but not checkable.** "Sure, make it nice then" with a nod is two people sharing a mood. Agreement without a done-condition is the original request wearing a handshake.
- **Restating intent in agent vocabulary.** A sentence the person has to translate back before they can agree with it has not been restated to them.
- **Clarifying instead of working.** A perfect instruction that arrives after the person could have had the artifact is a failure of the skill, not a success of the method.
